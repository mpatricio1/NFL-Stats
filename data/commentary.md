# NFL 2026 Stat Sheet: team write-ups through Week 7

Hand-written summaries shown at the top of each section of https://mpatricio1.github.io/NFL-Stats/ . Numbers come from data.json.


## Arizona Cardinals (ARI)

**Team efficiency.** Arizona won without scoring a touchdown. Chad Ryland kicked six field goals in an 18–16 win at Los Angeles, and the defense had its second-best game of the season: −0.14 EPA per play, 286 yards allowed and 3 sacks. The Cardinals are 2–4 at −52 (30th) and still allow 29.7 points per game, tied with New Orleans for the most. The offense is 17th in EPA per play (20th passing, 17th rushing); the defense is 27th, 30th against the pass but 7th against the run.

**DVOA.** DVOA has Arizona 22nd (−10.8%), up from 24th. The offense is 16th (−0.6%), the defense 27th (12.1%) and special teams tied for 11th (1.9%).

**Personnel & fronts.** Still a one-back offense: one back on 86% of plays (league 75%) and two or more on 10%, 2nd fewest. Arizona has the 6th-most tight ends on the field and, by FTN, the lowest play-action rate in the league (16% of dropbacks). The defense plays the 5th-fewest defensive backs and 7th-most linebackers and blitzes on 28% of dropbacks. Neither rush plan works: +0.20 EPA per dropback with four rushers (league +0.08) and +0.43 with five or more (league −0.01, 31st).

**Presnap motion.** Arizona moves before the snap on 62% of plays (10th; league 59%) and is worse when it does: −0.09 EPA per play with motion, +0.09 without. Runs show it most (−0.16 after motion, +0.17 without, 2nd best). The defense sees motion on 66% of plays (4th most) and is about average against it (−0.03), but without motion it is the worst in the league (+0.29, and +0.50 per dropback).

**Play action from under center.** Arizona uses play action from under center on 14% of dropbacks (25th; league 16%), and it works: +0.10 EPA per dropback against 0.00 on dropbacks without play action. The defense sees it on 19% of dropbacks, 4th most, and allows +0.32 (28th) against +0.22 without (29th).

**Play action from shotgun.** Shotgun play action is barely in the plan: 7 dropbacks all season (3%, 29th; league 7%), averaging 3.0 yards. Opponents have used it 11 times against Arizona for +0.55 EPA per dropback and 10.8 yards per play.

**Heavy sets.** Arizona goes heavy on 14% of plays (27th; league 20.5%) and loses value when it does: −0.15 EPA per play against 0.00 otherwise, with heavy-set runs averaging 2.7 yards (−0.25 EPA). The defense faces heavy sets more than anyone (31% of plays) and handles the runs well (−0.24 EPA per carry, 6th), but heavy-set passes hurt it (+0.39).

**Quarterbacks.** Jacoby Brissett is 23rd of 38 qualifiers at +0.01 EPA per dropback, with 9 touchdowns and 6 interceptions. At Los Angeles he completed 24 of 35 for 218 yards with no touchdowns and an interception, but he completed 8.1 points above expectation and wasn't sacked. For the season he is 5th in CPOE (+5.9) and has the 4th-quickest release (2.59 seconds). Blitzes still hurt: −0.24 EPA per dropback against extra rushers, +0.11 without.

**Running backs.** Jeremiyah Love had no carries in Week 6, so Tyler Allgeier and Bam Knight carried the load. Love is at 245 yards on 3.7 per carry (−0.39 under Next Gen Stats' expectation, 26th of 44 qualifiers). Allgeier has dropped to 3.0 per carry (−0.53, 34th) and −0.20 EPA per carry, while Knight has 112 yards on 18 carries. Through Week 6 the backs got 2.1 yards before contact (24th), and Arizona had 17 broken tackles (2.8 per game, tied for 12th), 5 by Allgeier.

**Receivers & TE.** Trey McBride still draws 29% of the targets: 49 catches for 361 yards and 3 touchdowns, though his efficiency has dipped to +0.19 EPA per target. Michael Wilson takes 38% of the air yards but averages 5.5 yards per target at −0.11 EPA. Kendrick Bourne (22 of 30, +0.35 EPA per target, 1.1 yards after catch over expectation) remains the efficient option, and Elijah Higgins has caught all 11 of his targets for 124 yards.

**Offensive line.** Pass protection held up at Los Angeles, where Brissett wasn't sacked. Through Week 6 Arizona was pressured on 21% of dropbacks (11th lowest), and the sack rate is 4.8% (8th). Elijah Wilkinson and Hjalte Froholdt have played every snap and Isaac Seumalo 99%, while Paris Johnson (65%), Jon Gaines (61%), Isaiah Adams and Jayden Williams (39% each) rotate. The middle is still the leak: 58 runs up the middle average 2.7 yards at −0.22 EPA, while runs off left end average 6.4.

**Who owns the sacks?.** No public stat measures this directly, because the time of each sack isn't released. The best free combination is pressure-to-sack rate (how often pressure becomes a sack, a measure that mostly reflects the QB) plus FTN's charting of each sack. Brissett wasn't sacked in Week 6, so the total stays at 12: 5 on extended plays, 4 when a standard rush won, 2 charged to him and 1 against a blitz. Through Week 6 he turned 23% of pressures into sacks (12 of 52), a little under the 27% league average.

**Front seven.** The run defense is 7th in EPA per rush (−0.14), but the pass rush is still thin by volume: 6.7 pressures per game through Week 6 (29th). It finishes when it gets there, with 14 sacks and a 7.3% sack rate (11th), including 3 at Los Angeles. Budda Baker has 3.5 sacks from safety and Josh Sweat 2; Sweat and Darius Robinson have 7 pressures each, and Walter Nolen and Baker 5.

**Secondary.** Still the weak spot: 30th in EPA per dropback allowed (+0.26). In PFR's player charting through Week 6, Budda Baker has allowed 257 yards and 3 touchdowns on 25 targets (111 passer rating), Mack Wilson 213 yards on 21 (132) and Denzel Burke 3 touchdowns (112) with an interception. Andrew Wingard remains the exception, with an interception, a forced fumble, 3 tackles for loss and a 67 rating allowed.

**Coverage.** Through Week 6, PFR's charting matches the EPA: a 108.1 passer rating allowed (30th; league 90.8) and 8.2 yards per target (30th), with 113 of 188 completed for 1,545 yards, 16 touchdowns (the most in the league) and 3 interceptions. Opponents complete only 60% (10th lowest), but the catches are big ones. Linebackers have fared worst (116.2 rating on 57 targets, 31st among linebacker groups), and safeties give up 12.0 yards per target (31st).

**Special teams.** Chad Ryland won the game at Los Angeles, 6 for 6 on field goals, and is now 14 of 16 with a 60-yarder (12 of 12 on extra points). Special teams DVOA ranks tied for 11th. Blake Gillikin nets 38.2 yards per punt with 8 of 28 inside the 20, and Devin Duvernay (28.2) and Jalen Brooks (26.2) both average over 26 yards per kickoff return.


## Atlanta Falcons (ATL)

**Team efficiency.** A second straight home loss, 31–23 to Chicago, drops Atlanta to 2–4 with a −11 point differential. The offense had its worst game since Week 2 (−0.31 EPA per play, a 30% success rate, 257 yards and three giveaways) and has slipped to 22nd in EPA per play and 14th in success rate, though the run game is still 3rd in EPA per carry. The defense was a little below average for a second week (+0.04 EPA per play allowed) and sits 17th overall and 9th against the run. Giveaways (12) are now more than double takeaways (5).

**DVOA.** DVOA dropped Atlanta to 24th overall (from 18th). The offense is 20th (from 17th), the defense 15th (from 12th), and special teams fell to 30th (from 27th).

**Personnel & fronts.** Still a run-first offense (30th in pass rate over expected) that keeps about 1.5 tight ends on the field, 8th most. Two-back looks make up 22% of plays (league 19%) and have produced +0.17 EPA per play (7th), against −0.16 with one back (29th). The defense rushes four on 72% of dropbacks (6th most) and blitzes on 26% by FTN's count (25th); four rushers (+0.09 EPA per dropback) and five or more (−0.05) are both close to the league rates (+0.08 and −0.01).

**Presnap motion.** Atlanta motions on 58% of plays (16th; league 59%). Dropbacks with motion are at −0.11 EPA against −0.23 without, the 9th-biggest passing gain in the league, while runs do about as well either way (+0.04 with, +0.06 without). On defense, Atlanta sees motion on 52% of snaps (27th) and is the league's best against runs without it (a 28% success rate and 2.9 yards a carry allowed, both 1st), but runs after motion gain 4.4 yards a carry (25th) with a 44% success rate (29th).

**Play action from under center.** Play action from under center is used on 17% of dropbacks (12th; league 16%) and has been the better call: −0.06 EPA and 8.4 yards per play (6th), against −0.24 EPA on dropbacks without play action (31st). It is a soft spot on defense, where opponents' under-center play action gains +0.18 EPA per dropback compared with −0.05 on their other dropbacks (9th).

**Play action from shotgun.** Shotgun play action is rare in Atlanta, 4% of dropbacks (25th; league 7%), but its 8 tries have produced +0.43 EPA per dropback (8th). The defense has seen it on only 13 dropbacks and allowed +0.83 EPA and 11.6 yards per play on them (29th), against −0.05 on dropbacks without play action.

**Heavy sets.** Heavy sets (two or more players in the backfield besides the quarterback, or an extra lineman reported eligible) are where Atlanta's offense works best. They make up 22% of plays (11th; league 20.5%) and produce +0.17 EPA per play (7th) and 6.4 yards (3rd), against −0.13 otherwise (28th), the 3rd-biggest gain in the league; heavy runs average 5.1 yards at +0.13 EPA (5th). The run defense is the reverse: heavy-set runs gain 4.9 yards and +0.03 EPA against Atlanta (27th), compared with 3.2 yards and −0.23 (3rd) on other runs.

**Quarterbacks.** Michael Penix Jr. threw 3 touchdowns against Chicago (19 of 33 for 225 yards, a 108.8 passer rating) but was sacked five times, four of them on blitzes. For the season he is 17th of 38 qualifiers at +0.10 EPA per dropback, with 6 touchdowns, 1 interception, a 106.0 rating, completions right at expectation (Next Gen Stats) and the quickest release in the league (2.54 seconds). Opponents blitz Atlanta on 42% of dropbacks, the most of any team, yet its passing has been worse without a blitz (−0.26 EPA per dropback, last) than against one (+0.01). Cooper Rush (−0.67 on 46 dropbacks) and Jack Strand (−0.60 on 19) keep the team 29th in passing EPA.

**Running backs.** Bijan Robinson is 2nd of 44 qualifiers in both yards per carry (5.8) and yards over Next Gen Stats' expectation (1.6 per carry), with 641 yards and a 50% success rate (3rd). Through Week 6 his 10 broken tackles (7 on runs) rank 6th. Brian Robinson remains a useful change of pace at +0.05 EPA per carry on 53 carries. Bijan's receiving is still low value: 20 catches for 144 yards and a touchdown at −0.42 EPA per target.

**Receivers & TE.** Drake London is the passing game: 31 catches on 46 targets for 550 yards, +0.62 EPA per target, 44% of the team's air yards and 2.3 yards after the catch above expectation (5th of 111), though he has yet to score. Kyle Pitts has 3 touchdowns among his 15 catches (169 yards, +0.26 EPA per target). Jahan Dotson gets the deep looks (16.6-yard average depth of target, 4th) and has 2 touchdowns, but has caught only 11 of 22 (−0.17 EPA per target). Austin Hooper has a touchdown among his 6 catches.

**Offensive line.** The protection numbers remain among the league's best: pressure on 18% of dropbacks through Week 6, 3rd lowest. The 8.4% sack rate (26th) is more about blitzes and the quarterbacks than the line; see Who owns the sacks. Bergeron and Neuzil have played every snap, Lindstrom 95%, Jawaan Taylor 93% and Jake Matthews 88%, and the offense draws the fewest penalties in the league (2.0 per game). Runs outside either end are the best plays (8.8 yards per carry on 22 to the left end, 8.3 and an 80% success rate on 15 to the right), while runs behind right guard succeed only 24% of the time.

**Who owns the sacks?.** No public stat measures this directly, because the time of each sack isn't released. The best free combination is pressure-to-sack rate (how often pressure becomes a sack, a measure that mostly reflects the QB) plus FTN's charting of each sack. It now points at the blitz as much as the quarterbacks. Chicago sacked Penix five times, four on blitzes and one on a standard four-man rush, and FTN charged none of them to him. Of Atlanta's 16 sacks, FTN puts 7 on blitzes (44%; league 25%), 4 on the quarterback, 4 on a standard rush and 1 on an extended play. Through Week 6, Cooper Rush turned 5 of 8 pressures into sacks (63%, 2nd highest of 41; league 27%) and Penix 9 of 19 (47%, 3rd highest).

**Front seven.** The run defense is 9th in EPA per rush (−0.13) and still the league's best against light boxes (−0.28 EPA per run with six or fewer in the box). The rush gets there without finishing: 11.7 pressures per game through Week 6 (4th) but a 5.6% sack rate (20th). Za'Darius Smith leads with 3.5 sacks and 5 tackles for loss in only 94 snaps; Cameron Thomas (12 pressures, 11 QB hits, 2 sacks) and Samson Ebukam (11 pressures, 2.5 sacks, 9 QB hits) lead the pressure count through Week 6, and Gervon Dexter has 1.5 sacks and 3 tackles for loss.

**Secondary.** 17th in EPA per dropback allowed. Through Week 6 the touchdowns are concentrated in two players: Mike Hughes has allowed 25 catches for 378 yards and 4 touchdowns (133 passer rating), and Jessie Bates III 4 touchdowns on 19 targets (133). C.J. Henderson has been the steadiest corner, with 6 passes defensed and an 85 rating allowed on 36 targets, and Avieon Terrell has allowed 7 catches on 14 targets. Xavier Watts has the unit's interception and a forced fumble.

**Coverage.** Through Week 6, Atlanta allows a 100.9 passer rating in coverage (24th; league 90.8). Opponents complete 64.6% of charted targets (25th) for 7.0 yards per target (14th) and have scored 13 touchdowns against 1 interception (tied for 26th and tied for 30th). Safeties are hit hardest: a 120.6 rating (27th) and 6 touchdowns on 56 targets. The corners are at 98.2 on 114 targets and the linebackers at 90.2, helped by Harold Perkins (12 of 24 for 95 yards).

**Special teams.** Down to 30th in special teams DVOA, and its 2.0 special teams penalties per game rank 29th. Nick Folk is 9 of 12 on field goals with a long of 51 and 13 of 13 on extra points. Jake Bailey nets 40.3 yards per punt with 8 of 21 inside the 20, and Zachariah Branch averages 10.4 yards on punt returns.


## Baltimore Ravens (BAL)

**Team efficiency.** 4–2 after a 13–10 loss at Cleveland with Tyler Huntley at quarterback again. The run game had its worst day of the season (−0.56 EPA per carry), and Baltimore managed 235 yards and a 33% success rate, wasting a defense that held the Browns to −0.12 EPA per play. For the season the offense is 7th in EPA per play (from 6th), 6th in success rate (from 2nd), 5th passing and 9th rushing (from 5th), scoring 26.0 points per game. The defense moved up from 14th to 11th, and the point differential is +24.

**DVOA.** DVOA drops Baltimore from 6th to 8th (14.1%), now three spots behind Cincinnati. The offense slipped from 3rd to 6th (14.1%), the defense from 16th to 17th (−1.8%), and special teams from 21st to 24th.

**Personnel & fronts.** Baltimore still runs more than its situations would predict (8 points below expected pass rate, 4th lowest) and has the 5th most tight ends on the field (1.7 per snap). The defense plays more defensive backs than any team (5.2 on average), so opponents see a light box on 60% of runs (3rd most; league 45%). Sending five or more rushers (−0.09 EPA per dropback) has beaten the four-man rush (0.00), and Baltimore blitzes at about the league rate (31% of dropbacks, FTN; league 32%).

**Presnap motion.** Baltimore motions on 65% of snaps (6th; league 59%), and motion still drives the run game: +0.09 EPA and 5.1 yards per run with motion (1st in EPA, 2nd in yards) against −0.21 and 4.0 without, the 2nd-biggest motion gain on runs. Passing is the reverse: +0.43 EPA per pass play without motion (best in the league) against +0.11 with it, the 2nd-largest drop-off. On defense, Baltimore is 7th against motion (−0.09 EPA per play) and 4th against passes with motion (−0.09).

**Play action from under center.** Baltimore uses play action from under center on 20% of dropbacks (6th; league 16%), and 89% of its under-center dropbacks include a fake. Those plays average +0.24 EPA against +0.16 on dropbacks without play action, a modest gain (14th). The defense rarely sees it (12% of dropbacks, 3rd least) but has been the best in the league against it: −0.47 EPA and 3.4 yards per play allowed.

**Play action from shotgun.** Shotgun play action is a small part of the offense (7% of dropbacks, 13th; league 7%), but it has been explosive: +0.76 EPA per play (2nd), 12.6 yards per play and an 86% success rate (best in the league) on 14 plays. That is a +0.60 gain over dropbacks without play action, 4th largest. On defense, Baltimore has allowed −0.61 EPA and 2.5 yards per play on 20 shotgun play-action dropbacks, 2nd best.

**Heavy sets.** Baltimore lines up in heavy sets (two or more backs besides the quarterback, or an extra lineman reporting eligible) on 25% of plays (8th; league 20.5%). Runs from those looks are the efficient ones: +0.10 EPA and 5.0 yards per carry against −0.10 and 4.6 from other sets, the 6th-biggest gain, and heavy-set plays overall average +0.15 EPA against +0.10. The defense has held opponents' heavy sets to −0.22 EPA per play (6th) and −0.55 on passes from them (2nd).

**Quarterbacks.** Lamar Jackson hasn't thrown a pass since Week 4, but his season still ranks among the best: 2nd of 38 qualified quarterbacks in EPA per dropback (+0.38), six touchdowns, one interception and completions 7.1 points above expectation (2nd of 36 by NGS). Tyler Huntley pushed the ball further at Cleveland (an 8.7-yard average depth, up from 4.8 at Atlanta) but went 19 of 31 for 165 yards and a touchdown, 8.1 points below his expected completion rate; on 85 dropbacks he is at +0.03 EPA per dropback (21st) and −1.8 by NGS (24th). As a team, play action (28% of dropbacks, 7th most) has been worth +0.38 EPA against +0.15 without.

**Running backs.** Derrick Henry has 534 yards on 130 carries (4.1 per carry), just above Next Gen Stats' expectation (+0.1 yards per carry, 16th of 44 qualified backs), but his EPA per carry is below break-even (−0.11) with a 42% success rate, and Baltimore's runs averaged −0.56 EPA at Cleveland. Justice Hill remains the efficient change-up: 133 yards on 18 carries (7.4 per carry) and +0.22 EPA per carry, best among the 74 runners with 15 or more carries. Through Week 6, Pro Football Reference charted six broken tackles for Henry (five on runs), four for Jackson and three for Zay Flowers, and Baltimore is 18th in broken tackles per game.

**Receivers & TE.** Zay Flowers leads in yards (471 on 42 targets, +0.63 EPA per target) and adds 2.0 yards of YAC over expectation per catch, 7th among 111 NGS-tracked receivers. Mark Andrews has the 2nd most targets (33, 20%) but 181 yards, −0.12 EPA per target and two drops on a 6.7-yard average depth. Rashod Bateman remains the downfield option (11.9-yard depth, +0.69 EPA per target), and tight ends Josh Cuevas and Matthew Hibner have caught 12 of 13 targets with a touchdown each. Baltimore's seven drops are below the league average of 8.9 (PFR).

**Offensive line.** Through Week 6 pressure arrived on 21% of dropbacks (14th lowest), but the sack rate has climbed to 7.1% (22nd) after three more sacks at Cleveland, none charged to Huntley by FTN. Olaivavega Ioane and John Simpson have played every snap and Roger Rosengarten all but two, while Ronnie Stanley (80%), Jovaughn Gwyn (44%), Emery Jones (44%) and Carson Vinson (25%) share the rest. Runs averaged 2.8 yards before contact through Week 6 (7th), and runs off right end are still the most reliable at a 62% success rate. Linemen have drawn 15 accepted penalties (2.5 per game, tied for 22nd).

**Who owns the sacks?.** No public stat measures this directly, because the time of each sack isn't released. The best free combination is pressure-to-sack rate (how often pressure becomes a sack, mostly a QB measure) plus FTN's charting of each sack. Through Week 6, Jackson turned 29% of pressures into sacks, close to the league's 27% (17th highest of 41), and FTN charged none of his seven sacks to him. Huntley's rate is higher (33%, tied with Jalon Daniels for 12th highest), but FTN put none of the three sacks at Cleveland on him after charging him with three at Atlanta. Of the team's 14 sacks, four came against blitzes, four on extended plays, three on a standard rush and three were the quarterback's fault.

**Front seven.** Baltimore sacked Deshaun Watson five times at Cleveland, lifting its sack rate from 21st to 16th (6.5%). Tavius Robinson leads with 3.5 sacks and eight QB hits, Mike Green has two sacks and four tackles for loss in 45% of snaps, and Trey Hendrickson two sacks in 41%. Through Week 6 the rush still lacked volume (8.5 pressures per game, tied with New England for 19th; league 9.4). Trenton Simpson and Kyle Hamilton lead in tackles for loss (six each), and the run defense slipped from 16th to 19th in EPA per rush.

**Secondary.** Pass defense climbed from 15th to 9th in EPA per dropback after holding Cleveland to −0.21 EPA per pass play. In coverage (Pro Football Reference, through Week 6), Trenton Simpson (a 48 passer rating on 16 targets, an interception) and Roquan Smith (51 on 24, an interception) lead, and Nate Wiggins has held 29 targets to 142 yards; Kyle Hamilton has four passes defensed and an interception. Malaki Starks (14 of 15 for 196 yards and two touchdowns, a 158 rating) and Marlon Humphrey (267 yards on 31 targets) are where opponents have found yards.

Linebackers are the strength at 49.5 on 44 targets with two interceptions, the best linebacker mark in the league (league 90.6). Safeties are the weak spot at 115.2 and 10.3 yards per target (league 94.7 and 8.3), and corners have allowed 93.8 (league 90.0).

**Special teams.** Tyler Loop is 10 of 13 on field goals with a 64-yarder and perfect on 16 extra points. Ryan Eckley nets 42.5 yards per punt with 13 of 26 inside the 20, and Chris Moore averages 28.6 yards on eight kick returns. Special teams slipped from 21st to 24th in DVOA, though the unit's five accepted penalties are tied for 5th fewest per game.


## Buffalo Bills (BUF)

**Team efficiency.** Buffalo is 5–1 after a 33–30 win at Las Vegas, its third straight game decided by three points. The offense is still 1st in EPA per play (+0.21), 2nd passing and 5th rushing, and leads the league in scoring (32.8 per game, a hair ahead of idle Detroit). The defense is still 30th in EPA per play and 28th in points allowed (28.5 per game) after giving up 378 yards. A clean game, with no giveaways, brought the turnover count to 9 against 6 takeaways.

**DVOA.** DVOA has Buffalo 4th overall (22.4%), down from 3rd. The offense is still 2nd and special teams climbed from 4th to 2nd, but the defense slipped again, from 27th to 28th.

**Personnel & fronts.** Buffalo uses more tight ends than any team (1.8 per snap, league 1.4) and fewer running backs than any (1.0). Empty sets take 12% of snaps (league 6%) at +0.24 EPA per play, and one-back sets also produce +0.24. On defense, neither rush plan works: four rushers allow +0.25 EPA per dropback and five or more +0.26 (league +0.08 and −0.01).

**Presnap motion.** Buffalo motions on 64% of snaps (7th; league 59%) and is the league's best offense with motion (+0.20 EPA per play) and 3rd without it (+0.22). Passes without motion remain the biggest weapon at 8.9 yards per pass, 1st. On defense, motion makes little difference: +0.13 EPA per play allowed with it (29th) and +0.12 without (28th).

**Play action from under center.** Buffalo calls play action from under center on 24% of dropbacks, 3rd most (league 16%), and it works: +0.32 EPA per play (5th) and a 56% success rate (4th). Dropbacks without it are just as good (+0.34, 2nd), so the gain is about zero (−0.02). Opponents use it against Buffalo more than against any other defense (22% of dropbacks) and average +0.30 EPA (27th).

**Play action from shotgun.** Shotgun play action is rare in Buffalo (7 dropbacks, 4%, 28th), but those seven have averaged +0.83 EPA and 13.3 yards, both 1st in the league. Opponents have used it 12 times against Buffalo for 4.0 yards per play (4th best).

**Heavy sets.** Buffalo is in heavy sets on 24% of plays (9th; league 20.5%), and they work: +0.10 EPA per play (10th) with a 53% success rate (4th), though the rest of the offense is better still (+0.24, 1st). He throws into tight windows less often than almost anyone (29th of 36), and neither play action nor the blitz changes much: +0.33 EPA per dropback with or without play action, +0.35 when blitzed and +0.32 when not.

**Running backs.** James Cook has 627 yards on 117 carries (5.4 per carry), 3rd in the league, and 0.9 yards per carry over Next Gen Stats' expectation (7th of 44 qualified backs), with a 50% success rate (2nd). Through Week 6 (Pro Football Reference), his eight broken tackles on runs are tied for 6th, and Buffalo is 3rd in yards before contact per carry (3.3; league 2.4). Khalil Shakir leads in targets (32) on a short 5.7-yard average depth of target, with 1.6 yards of YAC over expectation (12th of 111), and Dalton Kincaid leads in yards (315) with 1.9 YAC over expectation (9th).
